# iPhone widgets draw a timeline ahead and fetch sparingly

WidgetKit gives each widget a daily budget of reloads, and a reload may fetch. A widget that asked for a reload every minute on a trip would spend the day's budget by morning and then show nothing new until WidgetKit relented. So the widgets don't poll. Each reload fetches only when the answer kept in the app group is due (past its `staleAt`, or past the moment the server gave: `refreshAt`, else `nextChangeAt`), then writes a timeline of entries for every moment the widget looks different with nothing new: "Leave by" turning into "Leave now", the bus leaving, each stop passed on the ride (a second after the clock says so, as `Ride.passed` counts them), the sky's hour, and on Buses near you each bus going and each minute off its count. The next reload is asked for at the answer's next moment, never sooner than 5 minutes after now (`WidgetTiming.minReload`), never sooner than 15 s after the last fetch at a moment the server gave, 60 s without one, and after 15 minutes when the answer gives none. The app, a push and the background refresh reload the widgets whenever the card changes, so a sparse timeline costs nothing in freshness.

WidgetKit asks for every widget's timeline at once, so one fetch of the plan is shared by every widget asking (`BusProvider.refreshPlanOnce`), and an answer fetched in the last 15 s by anyone (the app, a push, another widget) isn't asked for again (`WidgetStore.recent`). A widget with no token doesn't reload, since signing in reloads it, except while the keychain is locked before the first unlock after a restart: then the token may be there unread, so it asks again in 5 minutes. Widgets of the same kind and size share one slot for their choice (Timetable, Nearby or a place), because WidgetKit doesn't say which of them was tapped; a place chosen on a widget goes back to the timetable after 30 minutes, or as soon as a trip has become due since it was chosen.

Buses near you shows clock times ("9:42", "~9:48" for a timetable's guess), not countdowns: a countdown stands still between entries and would be wrong most of the minute, while a clock time stays true. Under 15 minutes away the minutes go beside it, rounded down so a bus is never later than said ("6 min" from 6:59 to 6:00 away, "now" in its last minute; a timetable's time is "~1 min", never "now", which would pass for a bus at the stop), and each minute is an entry. A bus stays shown for a minute after it's due, as it may be at the stop or a little late. After 3 minutes (`WidgetMode.nearbyOld`) the times are dimmed, the minutes go, and the line above says the times may be old; a timetable's first bus never goes old. One board, `/api/me/nearby?stopped=1` with the phone's last place, is shared by every widget that shows buses near you (Buses near you, the Nearby chip on the others, the large Next bus widget's next buses), kept in the app group as it came and fetched at most once a minute by any of them (the server caches arrivals for 15 s). The large Next bus widget shows next buses only from a board under 30 minutes old, and only from a recent place of the phone's, not the home stop the server falls back to. Stops across a road from each other are a few metres apart, within GPS error, so the nearest stop can be the wrong side: the swap button puts the stop across the road first, and it stays swapped while the nearest stop is the same, for up to an hour. Both sides of the road on a large widget take up to eight rows a side, more than Android's five (a Glance column's limit), so a busy stop fills the room rather than leaving it bare.

Before the first answer a widget draws a made-up one in its place, redacted to bars, so it has the shape it will have; it doesn't shimmer, since widgets don't animate. Its ↻ and its buttons stay unredacted and work, its road has no bus (a made-up one would read as real, even greyed), and VoiceOver says what it's waiting for instead of the made-up words. The Apple Watch's complications follow the same rules (ADR 0016).

We considered countdowns on Buses near you, as the Nearby tab has, and rejected them for the reason above; reloading every minute on a trip, which spends the budget; and a board per widget, which would ask the server once for each widget on the screen for the same stops (rule 2).
