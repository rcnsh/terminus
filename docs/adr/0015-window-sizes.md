# Window width decides the layout, at 600 and 800, the same in every client

The clients were built for phones. On an iPad, an Android tablet, an unfolded iPhone Duo or a laptop's browser the phone layout stretched across the window, one column of rows running edge to edge, and the iPhone app didn't run on iPad at all. So every client now lays out by its window's width, at the same two widths: under 600 the phone layout; 600 to 799 the phone layout in a centred 560 column (the web app's column, which it had already); 800 and over two panes where a tab has a list and its pages (Stops, Settings) and the card beside Today on Now. The window's width rather than the device, because the same device gives very different windows: an iPad in Split View is phone-narrow, an unfolded Duo is tablet-wide, and a phone on its side is wider than it is tall. 800 rather than Material's 840 for the second step, because 840 leaves an 11-inch iPad in portrait (834) and a Pixel Tablet in portrait (800) in one column, where list and detail fit comfortably side by side. The tabs keep each platform's own large-screen look (iPad's tab bar or sidebar, Android's navigation rail from Material's 600, a rail on the web) while what's in the panes is the same everywhere, so the four clients still show the same thing the same way. We considered a third, wider step with three panes and a map beside Now, and rejected it for now as more than the screens need: text and controls keep their phone sizes, and the room goes to what sits beside them.
