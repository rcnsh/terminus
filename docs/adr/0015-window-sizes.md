# The window decides the layout: width at 600 and 800, height under 500, the same in every client

The clients were built for phones held upright. On an iPad, an Android tablet, an unfolded iPhone Duo or a laptop's browser the phone layout stretched across the window, one column of rows running edge to edge, and the iPhone app didn't run on iPad at all; on a phone on its side, Now's sky filled the screen and left no room for the answer. So every client lays out by its window, read live, and never by the device: the same device gives very different windows (an iPad in Split View is phone-narrow, an unfolded Duo is tablet-wide, a phone on its side is wide and short).

The width picks the layout, at the same two widths everywhere: under 600 the phone layout; 600 to 799 the phone layout in a centred 560 column (the web app's column, which it had already); 800 and over two panes where a tab has a list and its pages (Stops, Settings) and the card beside Today on Now. 800 rather than Material's 840 for the second step, because 840 leaves an 11-inch iPad in portrait (834) and a Pixel Tablet in portrait (800) in one column, where list and detail fit comfortably side by side. The tabs keep each platform's own large-screen look (iPad's tab bar or sidebar, Android's navigation rail from Material's 600, a rail on the web) while what's in the panes is the same everywhere, so the four clients still show the same thing the same way.

The height only makes the top of the page compact. A window under 500 tall is short: Now's horizon shrinks to the bands' hills, its wordmark joins the chips' row, its notices move under the card, and the bands on Stops and Settings lose their hills, so the answer is on screen without scrolling. The layout itself stays the width's, because one rule per axis is easier to keep identical across four clients than a table of width-and-height cases. 500 because the short windows that remain are 320 to 466 tall (a phone's browser on its side, Android split-screen, the iPhone Duo's outer panel in the tent position) and no tablet or laptop window that matters is anywhere near it.

Phones themselves stay upright in the apps, iPhone and Android alike, while tablets and open foldables turn. A phone on its side is about 400 tall, which even compact leaves room for only a step or two of the card; the answer is meant for a glance, and an upright phone shows it whole. The iPhone Duo is let through, since it stands on its side in the tent position. The web can't hold a browser upright, which is the main reason the short rule exists.

We considered a third, wider step with three panes and a map beside Now, and rejected it for now as more than the screens need: text and controls keep their phone sizes, and the room goes to what sits beside them. The full spec is in [window-sizes.md](../agents/window-sizes.md).
