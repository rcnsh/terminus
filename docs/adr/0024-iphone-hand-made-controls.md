# The iPhone app makes its own controls where the system's can't do the job

Most of the iPhone app is stock SwiftUI, and should stay so: the platform's look is what its users know (CLAUDE.md, "The interface explains itself"). In a few places the system's control can't show what the four clients show, and the app draws its own. Each looks like a candidate for simplifying back to the stock one; each was tried that way first.

**Pull to refresh stretches the sky.** `.refreshable` opens a gap above the content with a spinner in it, a grey edge under Now's sky and the Stops tab's band. The clients' pull is "the sky stretches": the room above the words grows, the gradient runs on to the horizon's new place, and a bus drives to the stop's sign as you pull (`Pull.swift` has the numbers and the motion, tested without SwiftUI in PullTests; `SkyPull.swift` draws it, as Android's `Pull.kt` and the web app do). So `PullHook` finds the screen's `UIScrollView` and adds a pan recogniser of its own: past the top, from a touch that began there, the finger's travel goes to the sky and the scroll view is put back where it was, so the page never bounces. Its handler runs after the scroll view's own, which has already moved the page for that event, and so it undoes that move. With VoiceOver on, the system's `.refreshable` stands in, since VoiceOver can drive it and can't drive a drag.

**The map's sheets are the app's own.** A tapped stop's or bus's sheet (`SheetFrame` in `MapSheets.swift`) is drawn over the map, not presented: the map stays live beside it, it floats in the bottom-left corner from 700 wide (ADR 0015), its footer's buttons stay in view while its rows scroll, and a new tap replaces it in place. A presented sheet does none of these. Its grab handle works as a sheet's does: dragged down it follows the finger and closes, a drag up gives a little and resists, and let go it carries on at the finger's speed (`Fling`, tested in FlingTests); a fling still on its way out when another sheet opens closes nothing. iOS 26's scroll-edge fade is turned off inside it, since the sheet is one surface.

**Two-way choices are segments of the app's own.** "This side | Across the road" and the pinned stops' names over their card (`Sides`, `PinSegments`, `Segment` in `BusesView.swift`) look like a segmented control, but a long stop name takes two lines there, which `Picker(.segmented)` can't.

**The status bar asks the screen.** SwiftUI's hosting controller takes the status bar's style from the whole app's colour scheme, but Now, Nearby, Stops and Settings start under a sky that can be dark in the light theme. `StatusBar.install()` replaces `preferredStatusBarStyle` on the hosting controller's class, once, with one that asks `StatusBar.light` first and falls back to the original. Outside the tabs (welcome, setup) and where the status bar sits beside the page (an iPhone Duo open, on a strip of the page's colour) it's the theme's.

**A page whose title is in the sky keeps the swipe back.** Hiding the navigation bar's title for the sky's takes the edge swipe back with it; `SwipeBack` gives the interactive pop gesture back to the stack.

**Before a system permission question, a neutral word.** The buttons that lead to the location and notification prompts say "Continue", never "Allow location": App Review rejects a button that answers the system's question for you.

We considered the stock controls in each case, and a UIKit sheet presentation for the map with a custom detent. Each either can't show the same thing the other clients show (the sky, a floating sheet, a two-line segment) or can't be used with VoiceOver; the hand-made ones are kept small, with their numbers in plain types that are unit tested.
