# The Apple Watch is a fifth client that shows a fixed subset of the phone

terminus's question is a glance (which bus, from which stop, when to leave), and a wrist is where a glance happens, so there's a watch app. Building a second, smaller product on the watch would mean a second set of decisions and words to keep in step with four clients that already show the same thing the same way, so it doesn't: the watch is a fifth client of the same server (ADR 0001) that shows a fixed subset of the phone (Now, Nearby, and the headline in its complications and Smart Stack) in the same words and order as the phone, and never anything the phone doesn't. Its code is the iPhone app's shared code built for watchOS (the answer's types, the API client, the countdown's words, the widgets' face and timing); its few words of its own are about setting it up and signing it out.

What stays on the phone: the map, the Stops tab, Settings beyond Sign out, onboarding, and the card's actions that change the plan (Not going, Not on campus today, Go later, Is this wrong?). Earlier and Later bus and Directions to the stop come along, because they're about the bus you're about to catch and take one tap.

It signs in by the phone, never by typing: the signed-in iPhone makes a pairing code and hands it to the watch over WatchConnectivity, and the watch redeems it for a session of its own, sending `x-terminus-client: ios-watch/<version>` (the iPhone app's platform, so the same minimum version and push rules, with its own flavour). So it shows in Devices and can be removed on its own, its Sign out signs out only itself, and the phone signing out leaves it signed in. An account without an email can make a code for its watch, which only a request from the watch can redeem.

It fetches on its own, by the iPhone widget's rules (`WidgetTiming`): one answer shared by the app and its complications, asked again at the answer's next moment, else after 15 minutes, never reloaded sooner than 5 minutes apart, and through the edge cache and the feed gate like every answer (rule 2). The countdowns tick on the watch between fetches.

We considered the watch reading the phone's answers over WatchConnectivity instead of fetching, and rejected it: the watch would be blank whenever the phone is in a bag, asleep or left at home, which is when a watch is worth having, and it would add a second path for the same answer. We also considered a code typed on the watch, and rejected it as slower and more error-prone than a tap on the phone that's already signed in.
